# The Daily Five: edition format (for the daily routine)

One file per day: `editions/YYYY-MM-DD.json`. Then run:
```
SITE_URL=https://readdailyfive.com python3 build.py
git add -A && git commit -m "Edition YYYY-MM-DD" && git push   # auth: credential helper reads $GITHUB_TOKEN
```
`build.py` renders the site, re-prices every past pick for the Scoreboard (Yahoo Finance chart API for stocks, Polymarket Gamma API for markets), writes `scoreboard.csv`, and freezes final exits in `data/exits.json`. Run it daily even with no new edition so the Scoreboard stays current.

## Rules
- Never invent facts, numbers, prices, or odds. Every item needs a real, resolving source URL.
- Entry prices must come from a real quote at publish time (Yahoo `https://query1.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/{SYM}?interval=1m&range=1d` → `meta.regularMarketPrice`; Polymarket `https://gamma-api.polymarket.com/markets?slug={slug}` → `outcomePrices[outcome]`).
- If an action is our own inference rather than the source's call, the `why` must say "Our read:".
- No third-party or personal strategy branding. No lottery/sweepstakes content.
- `symbol` must be a valid Yahoo ticker; `slug` + `outcome` must be a live Polymarket market (outcome = index into its `outcomes`).

- **Sports value rule (pick quality).** For every candidate game, read the DraftKings moneyline (ESPN scoreboard JSON above, or another real published line) and compute the edge: no-vig sportsbook chance minus the Polymarket price, in points. Prefer plays where Polymarket is **≥ 2 pts under** the no-vig line (verdict 💰 Value); fill the 5 with those first. A play within 2 pts either way is a **coin flip at a fair price** (🪙): use one only when there aren't 5 value plays, and its `why` must say so plainly, e.g. "Coin flip at a fair price: Polymarket 41¢ vs 41.5% no-vig at DraftKings; we like it because …". Never pick a side that is ≥ 2 pts **over** the line (⚠️ Pricey). Always fill `book` with the line you used. Why: through Oct 20, 14 settled picks bought within 2 pts of the books went 8-6 but averaged −5.5% per $1; a winning record at fair prices still loses money.

## Relaunch rules (from Oct 25, 2026 — SUPERSEDED for sports by the earnings-first rules above): Max Edge Day 1) — these override older rules below
Data behind them (beta, Oct 15–24, 42 settled sports picks, 16 settled 1-day stock calls):
- Sports under 40¢ went **2-13, avg −64% per $1**, even with a +2–3 pt "value" edge (13 picks at +2–3 pts went 3-10, −46%). A 2-pt edge on a longshot is inside the noise of the line.
- Coin flips (within 2 pts of the no-vig line) went 11-11, **−14%**.
- 40–60¢ picks went 8-6, **+10%**; that's the only bucket that made money.
- 1-day stock calls went 8-8, −0.3%: noise.

**Sports (pick quality gate). A pick qualifies only if ALL hold:**
1. Real DraftKings/ESPN (or other published) moneyline in `book`; no line, no pick.
2. Edge = no-vig book chance − Polymarket price **≥ +2.5 pts**.
3. Polymarket price **between 40¢ and 70¢** (no longshots, no heavy favorites).
4. No futures.
Publish **0–5** sports picks: only the ones that qualify. Fewer than 5 is fine; zero is fine ("No bets today: nothing cleared our bar" is a valid section). Never pad with coin flips.
**Bold Bet of the Day:** set `"bold": true` on exactly one qualifying sports pick, the one with the highest edge (tie → closer to 50¢). Max Edge's daily video leads with it. If nothing qualifies, no Bold Bet that day (Max says so).
**Max's lean (no-Bold-Bet days only; user-approved Oct 28, 2026).** When `sports` is `[]`, add a top-level `"lean"`: the closest-to-qualifying play from the same board, i.e. the game side with the **highest positive edge** (no-vig book chance − Polymarket price, in pts) among real games with a real published line, Polymarket **35¢–75¢** (the lean band, wider than the 40–70¢ pick band; user-approved Oct 28, 2026), no futures (tie → closer to 50¢). If no game has a positive edge in that price band, no lean (omit the field). **Line check (from Nov 3, 2026; leans were 1-4):** before posting, run `python3 lines.py <slug> <espn game url> <label> <entry>`. It reads the DraftKings open and current moneyline from ESPN and prints PASS only if the no-vig chance for our side has held or grown since open (within 0.5 pts). On SKIP, try the next-best qualifying game; if none passes, no lean that day (Max says the money moved the other way). Put the result in the lean as `"line_check":"PASS +0.6 pts since open (+150 to +145)"`. build.py shows every lean's book line (open, at Max's call, now/closed) on the lean box and a Book line move column on `/scoreboard/#leans`, so the guard is graded in public too. It is NOT a Bold Bet and NOT a pick: never set `bold`, never put it in `sports`, and the copy must call it a lean. Schema (same pricing fields as a sports pick):

  "slug":"mlb-mil-tb-2026-10-28","outcome":1,"entry":0.415,"event_date":"2026-10-28",
  "edge":1.2,"edge_at":"7:15 AM ET",

**Stocks: single 3-month horizon.** `horizon` must be `"3 Month"`. No 1 Day / 1 Month / 1 Year calls.
- Make **1–2 new calls per week** (not daily). On other days `finance` is `[]`; the site and email automatically show the open 3-month calls with live performance.

- Catalyst field (optional, on a 3-month call): `"catalyst": {"event","date":"YYYY-MM-DD","when":"after the close|before the open","right":[...],"wrong":[...],"max":"..."}`. Horizons close at entry date + 1 / 30 / 365 days (exit = first daily close on/after that date). A horizon ending on a weekend exits at the next session's close. A pick whose exit session is today freezes at today's close on the first build after 4:15 PM ET (not the next morning); today's still-forming daily bar is never frozen before 4:15 PM ET (until then the pick reads "live (exits at today's close)" or "live (exits at next close)"), and only when the daily bar matches Yahoo's `regularMarketPrice` (servers briefly disagree right after the close). Sports picks close when the Polymarket market resolves; on a real game (NFL/MLB/NBA/NHL slugs) a BUY settles provisionally at 100¢/0¢ as soon as Polymarket's events API reports the game ended with a decisive final score (note "final score, awaiting resolution", not frozen into exits.json, so the official resolution still wins). live.js does the same in the browser the moment a card's game-day line flips to FINAL. SELL finance returns are short-side; sports picks are always BUY of an outcome (use the NO outcome to fade).

## Live prices (automatic)
GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/pages.yml`) rebuilds and redeploys every 15 min on weekday market hours (13–21 UTC, at :07/:22/:37/:52) and every 30 min otherwise (:12/:42). GitHub cron is best-effort and can lag or skip; `live.js` shows a "stale, refreshing soon" note when `live.json` is >25 min old in market hours (>50 min otherwise). Kick a manual rebuild with `gh workflow run pages.yml --repo gregpunch/daily-five`. Each build writes `site/data/live.json` (cur, day open, since-open %, since-pick %, final, label, updated_et, `ts` epoch). The homepage and Scoreboard show a "Today so far" strip (open picks up/down, avg since pick, avg today, best/worst) that live.js recomputes every minute. `live.js` reloads it every 60 s and also polls Polymarket's Gamma API directly (CORS-enabled) for sports cards. Stocks are labeled delayed (Yahoo may lag up to ~15 min). After 4 PM ET on weekdays the strip flips to "🔔 Closing bell" (amber; "stocks closed for the day · sports still live"), shows "Pre-market" before 9:30 AM ET and "Weekend" on Sat/Sun, lists picks from the latest edition that have settled (final P/L), and counts down to the next 7 AM ET edition (also in the recap box). Resolved Polymarket markets drop out of Gamma's default query, so `build.py` retries with `&closed=true`; a closed market only counts as final (and is frozen into `data/exits.json`) once its price is ≥0.99 or ≤0.01. `site/` is no longer committed: CI builds it. Only `data/exits.json` is committed back by CI (with `[skip ci]`).

## Daily Five Score (computed by build.py)
Four components, each 0–25 (neutral 12.5 when they can't be computed, and the card says so):
1. **Credibility**: hit rate of `source.name` across closed picks in our Scoreboard (needs ≥5 closed picks; otherwise "new source").
2. **Consensus**: from the `consensus` field, which you fill with REAL sources found by search: `"consensus": {"agree": [{"name","url"}], "dissent": [{"name","url"}]}` (count the primary source in `agree`).
3. **Edge**: stocks = % gap to Yahoo `financialData.targetMeanPrice` (signed by BUY/SELL). Sports = no-vig sportsbook probability minus Polymarket price, in points, from `"book": {"name","url","odds":[american_for_our_outcome, american_other]}` (real published lines only).
   Sportsbook lines (automatic, `attach_books`): for NFL/NBA/MLB/NHL/WNBA game picks, build.py reads the moneyline from ESPN's free public scoreboard JSON (`site.api.espn.com/apis/site/v2/sports/{sport}/{league}/scoreboard?dates=YYYYMMDD`, DraftKings, no account or key), matched on start time (±4h) and team name, and stores it in `data/books.json` (key `date/sN`, with read time). Before the game starts, Edge = live ESPN no-vig line vs live Polymarket price (refreshed every build). Once the game starts or settles, Edge = the edition's `book` (line at publish) vs our entry, else the last pre-game ESPN line stored in `data/books.json` vs our entry (labeled "Pre-game line" with its read time). Futures keep the edition `book` vs the live price. Keep filling `book` in editions (it's the publish-time line and covers futures). Sports cards show a "📒" sportsbook-check line (odds, no-vig %, Polymarket price, edge in points, source + time); pick pages show the full math and any other line we have. Every sports card, pick page and the email also show a plain **value verdict** from that edge (`vkey`/`verdict` in build.py, `VAL_PTS`=2): 💰 Value (≥ +2 pts), 🪙 Coin flip at a fair price (within 2 pts), ⚠️ Pricey (≤ −2 pts), ❔ Unchecked (no real line); started/settled picks keep the verdict at our entry, past tense, plus our settled record for that verdict. The home/edition sports header has a "Value check vs the sportsbooks" summary of the open plays. `/sports-results/` has a "Results by value verdict" section (`#value`) with each verdict's settled record and avg, and tags every settled row with its verdict; `/how-we-rank/#value` explains it. `data/books.json` is committed by local builds (edition pushes, check-ins), not by CI, so CI never races the edition push; CI reads the live line in memory each build.
4. **Risk/reward**: stocks = horizon-scaled target gap ÷ (60-day daily vol × √trading days); sports = EV at the fair probability.
Cards show the score badge, four component bars, and a one-line breakdown. Finance and sports are ranked by score (#1 = highest); the hero "Top call" is the top-scored finance pick (so `top_call` text should match it, or omit `top_call`).

### Weekly earnings recaps (`/earnings/week-of-<monday>/`)

**Was it priced in? (from Nov 10, 2026).** Results recaps add a "3 mo. into it" column (each explainer's `prerun()` 3-month move to the last close before the report, stored as `r['_pr']`) and a `#priced-in` card: how many stocks that ran up 10%+ going in fell after the report (and how many of those beat on EPS), and how many that fell 10%+ going in bounced, each name linked with both moves, plus a matching FAQ. Real Yahoo closes only; names missing either number are left out and counted.
`earnings_explainer.week_recaps()` builds one page per Monday-start week from the explainer JSON (weekend reports such as Berkshire's Saturday release roll into that week and stretch the label, e.g. "Nov 2–7, 2026"). Each page: EPS/revenue beat tallies, biggest pop/drop and beat-but-fell count (live moves from `render()`), a table of every report with EPS / revenue / stock move, signup, FAQ (FAQPage schema), prev/next week links. `/earnings/` lists all weeks (`#weekly`), and every quarter explainer links to its week's recap. Numbers come only from the JSON and live moves; nothing is typed in by hand.
**Preview weeks (`data/calendar/week-of-<monday>.json`).** A calendar file (`week`, `fetched`, `source` {name,url}, optional `featured` tickers for the summary, `items` [{date, ticker, company, quarter, when, eps_est}]) copied from a public earnings calendar gives that week its recap URL before any explainer exists: a preview page "Earnings next week (…): who reports and what Wall Street expects" with an "On the calendar" table (date, before/after the bell, EPS estimate, source + fetched date). As explainers publish, the same URL becomes the results recap and the calendar table shrinks to names not yet explained (matched by ticker). The prior week's recap shows a "Coming up next week" link while the next week has no results. Add next week's file every Saturday check-in. The homepage (`home_weeks`) and the 7 AM email (`week_roundup` in email_build.py) link the current week's recap and the next week's preview; labels flip on Monday so the email reads "Last week in earnings" plus "who reports this week."

**Upcoming ticker pages (`upcoming_hubs`).** Every calendar name we haven't explained yet gets `/earnings/<ticker>/` as an earnings-date page ("<Company> (<TK>) earnings date: <quarter> on <date>, EPS estimate"): report date, before/after the bell, quarter, consensus EPS, the calendar source + fetched date, same-day reporters (linked), signup, FAQ. Only calendar fields are shown, nothing invented. Before the report date it also shows "How <TK> stock is trading into the report" (`runup()`/`runup_section()`): latest price, past week/month/3-month change, S&P 500 3-month change and the 3-month range, all from Yahoo's chart API at build time; omitted if the fetch fails. When that company's explainer JSON lands, `ticker_hubs` writes the full hub at the same URL. Local quick build: `SKIP_PNG=1 python3 build.py` skips headless-Chrome share images (Chrome runs have a 30s process-group kill timeout).

**Evergreen earnings calendar (`calendar_page`).** `/earnings-calendar/` is one fixed URL ("earnings calendar this week") showing the current Mon–Fri week from data/calendar (from Saturday on, the coming week), day by day, grouped before the open / after the close, with the EPS estimate. Explained names show beat/miss + stock move and link to the explainer; the rest link to their `/earnings/<ticker>/` date page. Linked from the homepage week cards and /earnings/#weekly. Needs each Saturday's calendar file to stay current. Upcoming rows also show a **Past month** column (stock's ~21-trading-day change from `runup()`, Yahoo chart API at build time; '—' if the fetch fails or the name is already explained). A **Biggest moves into earnings this week** card (`#movers`) sits under the hero: top 3 run-ups and top 3 sell-offs by real 3-month change (`runup()['m3']`), only names that haven't reported yet (today's before-open names and, after 4 PM ET, today's after-close names are dropped), with the S&P 500's 3-month change and a matching FAQ. Omitted with fewer than 4 priced names.

- **Up next card** (`up_next()` in earnings_explainer.py): every quarter explainer and ticker hub ends with "Up next: reporting <today/tomorrow/weekday>" from data/calendar (before the open first, EPS est.; explained names show beat/miss), linking `/earnings-calendar/#d-<date>`.

**Run-up into the report (`prerun()`/`prerun_html()`).** Each quarter explainer's reaction card adds an "Into the report" line: the stock's change over the ~63 trading days ending at the last close before the report (report-day close for after-the-close reporters), the S&P 500 over the same window, and whether the reaction extended or reversed it ("ran up 37.7% ... then the report reversed it: -9.4%"), with a one-line "high bar / low bar" note when the run-up was 10%+ either way and the reaction went the other way. Daily closes from Yahoo's chart API at build time; omitted if the fetch fails. Also adds the FAQ "How much did <TK> stock move before earnings?".
- **/earnings/#priced-in (season tracker):** `priced_in_season()` in earnings_explainer.py, called from `index_section()`. Uses every explained report with both `_pr` (3-month run-up, Yahoo) and a live `_move`: tiles for "X of Y hot stocks (10%+ run-up) fell after reporting", how many of those beat EPS, and "X of Y beaten-down (−10%+) bounced"; a table of average post-report move by bucket (ran up 10%+ / less than 10% / fell 10%+); top 6 examples each. Shown only with 3+ hot/cold names. Sets `PRICED_FAQ`, which `earnings_hub()` in build.py appends to the /earnings/ FAQ + FAQPage schema.
- **Season bucket notes (`season_note()` in earnings_explainer.py):** `render()` stores the explained reports in `B['_ERPTS']`; for a stock up 10%+ (or down 10%+) over 3 months into its report, the upcoming earnings-date page's "trading into the report" card adds "This season, X of Y stocks we explained that ran up 10%+ going in fell after reporting (avg move …)" (or the beaten-down bounce rate) with a link to `/earnings/#priced-in`. /earnings-calendar/ upcoming rows tag those names "🔥 3 mo. +N% · high bar" / "🧊 3 mo. −N% · low bar" under Past month, and the #movers card cites the same season stats. Needs 3+ reports in the bucket; the stock itself is excluded.


**Email On deck high/low bar tags (Nov 10, 2026).** `on_deck()` in email_build.py tags each reporter whose real 3-month move into the report (Yahoo daily closes via `earnings_explainer.runup`) is +10% or more (🔥 high bar) or −10% or worse (🧊 low bar), plus a one-line legend linking /earnings/#priced-in. Omitted on any fetch failure or a smaller move. The calendar link puts the UTM query before the `#d-<date>` fragment.

**Big-move titles + FAQ (Nov 11, 2026).** When a quarter explainer's live move vs the pre-report close is ±`BIG_MOVE` (8%) or more and there's no beat/miss mismatch (`surprise()` is None), `render()` titles the page "Why <TK> stock jumped/sank N% after <Company> <quarter> earnings", puts the move in the meta/Article description, and leads the FAQ with "Why did <TK> stock jump/sink after earnings?" (live move, top two headline numbers vs consensus, Max's verdict). Other explainers with a known move get it in the meta description. All numbers come from the live Yahoo reaction at build time, so keep `verdict_word` free of price moves (no "stock up ~16% premarket"; it goes stale).

**Day-after scorecard (email, from Nov 12, 2026).** Weekday emails (not `lead_week` editions) get a one-line "🗓 Day-after scorecard" under Earnings, explained (`day_after_line()` in email_build.py, `day_after()` in earnings_explainer.py): every explainer whose first full trading session (report day if before the open, else the next weekday) was the last weekday before the edition date, with that session's real Yahoo daily close vs the stored `prev_close`. It settles last night's premarket numbers (e.g. CoreWeave +24.7% at the Nov 11 close). Omitted when empty or Yahoo fails; no manual fields needed.

- **Home Day-after scorecard (Nov 11, 2026):** `home_dayafter()` adds a 🗓 line under the home "Earnings, explained" scoreline: the latest session's first-day reporters, real Yahoo close vs pre-report close. After 4:15 PM ET on weekdays the just-closed session counts. Automatic; nothing to fill in editions.

**Day-one close on explainers (`day_one()`).** Once a report's first full trading session has closed (report day if before the open, else the next weekday; after 4:15 PM ET), each explainer's reaction card leads with "Day one: TK closed +X% on <day>" (real Yahoo daily close vs the stored `prev_close`) and adds the FAQ "How did TK stock close the day after earnings?". Settled closes are cached in `data/dayone.json`, committed by local builds; CI fetches any missing ones in memory.
